[scene: 1. The problem]
[music: start]
NARRATOR: Your immune system is brilliant. But the first time it meets a new germ, it's slow.
NARRATOR: Building the right defence can take a week or more. And a week is a long time to be sick.
[scene: 2. The rehearsal]
NARRATOR: A vaccine is a rehearsal. It shows your body a harmless piece of the germ, or instructions to make one, so it can practise without the danger.
[scene: 3. The memory]
NARRATOR: Your body answers with antibodies, and with memory cells that keep the germ's shape on file, often for years.
[scene: 4. The real thing]
NARRATOR: So when the real germ arrives, those memory cells know it at once. A defence that once took a week now starts within days, often before you feel ill at all.
STUDENT (surprised): Wait. So the vaccine doesn't fight the germ itself?
NARRATOR (happy): Exactly. It trains the team that does.
[scene: 5. Everyone else]
NARRATOR: And when enough people are protected, a germ struggles to spread at all. That shields the people who can't be vaccinated, like newborn babies.
[music: out]
